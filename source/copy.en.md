**Translator's notes on the choices that carry meaning:**

- Slovenian formal *vi* has no English equivalent. Rendered as plain "you", kept warm rather
  than stiff — the source is intimate, not corporate.
- Slovenian **dual** verbs (*spoznava se*, *ustvarjava*, *raziskujeva*) mean *the two of us
  specifically*, not a general "we". Where the intimacy carries weight it is rendered as
  "the two of us"; elsewhere as "we", which in context reads as the pair in the room.
- The register deliberately avoids the coaching-industry vocabulary she is distancing
  herself from — no "unlock", "potential", "journey", "transformation", "empower".
- Her short one-line paragraphs are a rhythm, not an accident. Line breaks are preserved.
- British spelling throughout (*recognise*, *realise*), consistent with a European practice.

## HOME

### Hero

A space where you can set every answer down for a moment.

Coaching for the times when you know something is shifting, but not yet quite where it is
taking you.

Room for a clearer view of yourself, your decisions, your patterns and your next steps.

### Opening question

What might you hear, if you truly let yourself listen to yourself?

In the pace of ordinary days we tend to look for answers outside ourselves.
In advice. In expectations. In what we ought to be doing. In what we have always done.

Coaching makes a different kind of space.

A space where you can stop, look a little deeper, and begin to recognise what, right now,
is genuinely yours.

### Perhaps this is where you are

Something in your life is moving.
Perhaps it has no name yet. Perhaps you have known it a long time.

**Facing an important decision**
Several paths are open to you, and none of them feels entirely clear.

**In the middle of a change**
What once worked no longer holds you up. And the new has not taken shape yet.

**Inside an old pattern**
The situation is different, the feeling familiar. Something keeps repeating.

**Successful, but not quite in step with yourself**
Seen from outside, much is in place. Inside, something is asking for attention.

**At a crossroads, in work or in life**
You are asking what you want to keep, what to let go of, and who you want to be in the
next chapter.

**Carrying more responsibility**
You lead people, make demanding decisions or carry a great deal, and you miss having
somewhere to think without pressure.

### What coaching is

It is not about someone telling you what to do.

I believe people often hold more answers than they realise.

Sometimes those answers are drowned out by fear, by old habits, by the expectations around
us, or by ways of operating that once helped and may now be holding us back.

Coaching is a space where you can begin to notice this.

Through conversation, questions, listening and reflection, the two of us gradually build
more clarity about what is happening, what supports you, what holds you back and where you
want to go next.

[More about coaching]

### What coaching can give you

More awareness. More choice.

**A clearer view**
Once things are said aloud and looked at from several angles, they often take a different
shape.

**Deeper self-awareness**
You come to recognise your values, needs, beliefs, reactions and recurring patterns more
clearly.

**More conscious decisions**
Not out of reflex or other people's expectations, but with more understanding of what
matters to you.

**New perspectives**
A question can open up possibilities that our settled way of thinking makes hard to see.

**Real movement**
An insight matters most when it can be carried into a life, a relationship or a decision.

**More contact with yourself**
Not so that you become someone else, but so that you can act more in keeping with who you
already are.

### How I work

A quiet space. An honest conversation. No need for perfect answers.

What matters to me is not to see you only through the goal, or the difficulty, you arrive
with.

I am interested in the whole person.

Your experience. Your values. Beliefs. Feelings. Relationships. Patterns. The way you make
decisions, and the way you have learned to move through the world.

In my work I:
– listen without judgement and without rushing towards a solution,
– stay curious, including where the answer is not immediately clear,
– ask questions that open new perspectives,
– point out the patterns and the things I notice as we talk,
– respect your pace and your particularity,
– do not decide in your place,
– support you in arriving at your own answers.

Because depth does not have to mean pressure.

I had to begin by listening to myself, too.

My path into coaching was not only a decision about a new career.

It was also a personal move away from a way of living in which I long believed I had to be
strong, have the answers, and above all keep going.

The work on myself showed me something different.

That change often does not mean becoming someone else. Sometimes it means slowly setting

That experience still shapes the way I work with people.

## COACHING

### Hero

You do not need to have everything clear in order to begin.

Coaching is a space for exploring what matters to you right now.

### What coaching is

A process in which you start to hear yourself more clearly.

I understand coaching as a safe, confidential, developmental process.

It does not change a person from the outside, and it offers no universal recipes.

It lets you step back for a moment from the pace of your days, from expectations and from
acting on reflex, and look at your situation with a little more distance.

In conversation we can open up questions that are hard to put to yourself alone.

- What is really going on?
- What matters to you?
- What keeps repeating?
- What supports you?
- What limits you?
- What do you need?
- What would you choose, if you took other people's expectations out of the equation for a
  moment?

The aim is not a perfect answer.

The aim is more clarity, more awareness, and the chance of a more conscious choice.

### My philosophy

Answers are not always absent. Sometimes they are only covered over.

I believe every person carries a great deal of wisdom, strength and answers within them.

Sometimes we struggle to hear them, because they are covered over by fears, old patterns,
expectations, past experience, or identities that once helped us and may no longer serve us.

For me, change begins with awareness.

Once we can see something, we can form a different relationship to it.

And once the relationship is different, we have more room to choose.

### What coaching is not

I do not advise you how to live your life.

Coaching is not:
– advice-giving,
– mentoring,
– therapy,
– diagnosis,
– handing over ready-made solutions,
– persuading you what is right for you.

As a coach I do not take on the role of someone who knows your life better than you do.

What I can do is be alongside you, so that together we make a space in which you can see it
more clearly yourself.

### How I work

The person before the goal.

Goals matter, but on their own they do not tell the whole story.

So alongside your goal, I am interested in who you are in relation to it.

- How you think.
- What you feel.
- What you are afraid of.
- Which values matter to you.
- Where the old patterns appear.
- What happens just before a decision.
- What you do when things become uncomfortable.
- What is yours, and what you may have been doing largely because it was expected of you.

Coaching then becomes more than a conversation about a goal.

It becomes an exploration of the way you are bound up with it.

### Presence and listening

What stays between the words matters too.

In my work I do not listen only to the content.

I also pay attention to repeated words, shifts in tone, feelings, energy, silence, and the
moments when something in the conversation changes.

Sometimes the question worth exploring lies exactly there.

Not so that I can tell you what something means.

But so that together we can look at what it means to you.

### Working together

**01. Introductory conversation**
First we meet.
You tell me what brought you to coaching, what you need at the moment and what you expect.
I describe how I work and answer your questions.
The point of the first conversation is mainly to see whether we want to work together.

**02. Setting the focus**
Together we clarify what you want to give the coaching to.
Sometimes the goal is clear from the start.
Other times the real question only emerges through the conversation.
Either is fine.

**03. Coaching sessions**
Sessions rest on conversation, listening, reflection and questions.
As we go we explore your thinking, your reactions, your values, your inner patterns and the
possibilities you may not see yet.

**04. Carrying it into life**
Coaching does not stay inside the room.
We also explore what your insights will mean in practice, and how you want to carry them
into your decisions and your everyday life.

**05. Reflecting on the process**
As we go we check what is changing, what is helping you and what needs a different kind of
attention.
We adapt the process to you, not the other way round.

### Ethics and confidentiality

A professional relationship begins with clear boundaries.

I understand confidentiality as the ground the coaching relationship stands on.

It also matters to me that you know what you can expect from coaching, what is my
responsibility and what remains yours.

I respect your decisions and your autonomy.

I do not take on the role of a therapist, an adviser, or someone who would know better than
you.

For me, an important part of responsible coaching is continually reflecting on my own work,
knowing the limits of my competence, and being willing to seek professional support or
point you elsewhere when coaching is not the right kind of help.

### CTA

The beginning can be just a conversation.

You do not need to know how many sessions you need, or to name precisely what you want to
change.

To begin, it is enough to know that you want to look at something differently.

## ABOUT ME

### Hero

My path to coaching did not begin with coaching.

It began with a sense that the way I had long lived and worked was no longer the whole
answer.

### My story

For a long time I built a life and a career inside an identity that gave me a great deal.

I knew how to be strong. To take responsibility. To keep going. To make sure things worked.

For a long time I believed that this was exactly what I had to be.

At the same time, the wish to work with people more deeply never really went away.

Choosing coaching therefore meant far more than a change of profession.

It also meant being willing to look at myself.

### When you start to see yourself differently

Training as a coach did not only teach me methods and questions.

A large part of the process was the work on myself.

I began to recognise my own reactions, beliefs, old patterns, and the ways I had learned
over a lifetime to protect or adapt myself.

Exploring unconscious patterns and archetypes mattered to me particularly.

In time I came to understand how hard it is to truly accompany another person if I am not
willing to look into my own inner workings.

That understanding became one of the foundations of my work.

### A change that is not becoming someone else

Early on my path, I had the feeling I was turning into a different person.

Today I see it differently.

I was not becoming someone else.

Step by step I was coming closer to a part of myself that had long been covered over by
roles, expectations and ways of operating that I had once needed.

So in my work with people I do not start from the idea that they need "fixing".

The question that interests me far more is this:

What can change, once you begin to understand yourself better?

### Why coaching

Coaching drew me in because I experienced for myself how valuable it is to have a space
where nobody is trying to correct you.

A space where you do not have to know straight away.

Where you can say something and truly hear it for the first time.

Where someone is with you with enough presence that you begin to recognise your own
answers.

That is the kind of space I want to make too.

### How I want to be alongside a client

**Present.**
Listening, rather than preparing my reply while you speak.

**Curious.**
Not assuming I know what something means to you.

**Honest.**
Able to name what I notice, even when it opens a harder question.

**Respectful.**
Of your story, your pace and your decisions.

**Accountable.**
Knowing the limits of my work, and continuing to develop myself as a coach.

### Something I believe

Change rarely happens overnight.

More often it comes slowly.

Layer by layer.

First we notice something.

Then we understand it.

Then we form a different relationship to it.

And one day, in a situation where we would once have done the same thing automatically, we
find that we have a choice.

That moment seems to me one of the most valuable parts of personal development.
