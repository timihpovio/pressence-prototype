My story

For a long time I built a life and a career inside an identity that gave me a great deal.

I knew how to be strong. To take responsibility. To keep going. To make sure things worked.

For a long time I believed that this was exactly what I had to be.

At the same time, the wish to work with people more deeply never really went away.

Choosing coaching therefore meant far more than a change of profession.

It also meant being willing to look at myself.

When you start to see yourself differently

Training as a coach did not only teach me methods and questions.

A large part of the process was the work on myself.

I began to recognise my own reactions, beliefs, old patterns, and the ways I had learned over a lifetime to protect or adapt myself.

Exploring unconscious patterns and archetypes mattered to me particularly.

In time I came to understand how hard it is to truly accompany another person if I am not willing to look into my own inner workings.

That understanding became one of the foundations of my work.

A change that is not becoming someone else

Early on my path, I had the feeling I was turning into a different person.

Today I see it differently.

I was not becoming someone else.

Step by step I was coming closer to a part of myself that had long been covered over by roles, expectations and ways of operating that I had once needed.

So in my work with people I do not start from the idea that they need “fixing”.

The question that interests me far more is this:

What can change, once you begin to understand yourself better?

Why coaching

Coaching drew me in because I experienced for myself how valuable it is to have a space where nobody is trying to correct you.

A space where you do not have to know straight away.

Where you can say something and truly hear it for the first time.

Where someone is with you with enough presence that you begin to recognise your own answers.

That is the kind of space I want to make too.

How I want to be alongside a client

Present

Listening, rather than preparing my reply while you speak.

Curious

Not assuming I know what something means to you.

Honest

Able to name what I notice, even when it opens a harder question.

Respectful

Of your story, your pace and your decisions.

Accountable

Knowing the limits of my work, and continuing to develop myself as a coach.

Something I believe

Change rarely happens overnight.

More often it comes slowly.

Layer by layer.

First we notice something.

Then we understand it.

Then we form a different relationship to it.

And one day, in a situation where we would once have done the same thing automatically, we find that we have a choice.

That moment seems to me one of the most valuable parts of personal development.
