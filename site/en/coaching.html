What coaching is

A process in which you start to hear yourself more clearly.

I understand coaching as a safe, confidential, developmental process.

It does not change a person from the outside, and it offers no universal recipes.

It lets you step back for a moment from the pace of your days, from expectations and from acting on reflex, and look at your situation with a little more distance.

In conversation we can open up questions that are hard to put to yourself alone.

  • What is really going on?
  • What matters to you?
  • What keeps repeating?
  • What supports you?
  • What limits you?
  • What do you need?
  • What would you choose, if you took other people's expectations out of the equation for a moment?

The aim is not a perfect answer.

The aim is more clarity, more awareness, and the chance of a more conscious choice.

My philosophy

Answers are not always absent. Sometimes they are only covered over.

I believe every person carries a great deal of wisdom, strength and answers within them.

Sometimes we struggle to hear them, because they are covered over by fears, old patterns, expectations, past experience, or identities that once helped us and may no longer serve us.

For me, change begins with awareness.

Once we can see something, we can form a different relationship to it.

And once the relationship is different, we have more room to choose.

What coaching is not

I do not advise you how to live your life.

Coaching is not:

  • advice-giving,
  • mentoring,
  • therapy,
  • diagnosis,
  • handing over ready-made solutions,
  • persuading you what is right for you.

As a coach I do not take on the role of someone who knows your life better than you do.

What I can do is be alongside you, so that together we make a space in which you can see it more clearly yourself.

How I work

The person before the goal.

Goals matter, but on their own they do not tell the whole story.

So alongside your goal, I am interested in who you are in relation to it.

  • How you think.
  • What you feel.
  • What you are afraid of.
  • Which values matter to you.
  • Where the old patterns appear.
  • What happens just before a decision.
  • What you do when things become uncomfortable.
  • What is yours, and what you may have been doing largely because it was expected of you.

Coaching then becomes more than a conversation about a goal.

It becomes an exploration of the way you are bound up with it.

Presence and listening

What stays between the words matters too.

In my work I do not listen only to the content.

I also pay attention to repeated words, shifts in tone, feelings, energy, silence, and the moments when something in the conversation changes.

Sometimes the question worth exploring lies exactly there.

Not so that I can tell you what something means.

But so that together we can look at what it means to you.

Working together

01

Introductory conversation

First we meet. You tell me what brought you to coaching, what you need at the moment and what you expect. I describe how I work and answer your questions. The point of the first conversation is mainly to see whether we want to work together.

02

Setting the focus

Together we clarify what you want to give the coaching to. Sometimes the goal is clear from the start. Other times the real question only emerges through the conversation. Either is fine.

03

Coaching sessions

Sessions rest on conversation, listening, reflection and questions. As we go we explore your thinking, your reactions, your values, your inner patterns and the possibilities you may not see yet.

04

Carrying it into life

Coaching does not stay inside the room. We also explore what your insights will mean in practice, and how you want to carry them into your decisions and your everyday life.

05

Reflecting on the process

As we go we check what is changing, what is helping you and what needs a different kind of attention. We adapt the process to you, not the other way round.

Ethics and confidentiality

A professional relationship begins with clear boundaries.

I understand confidentiality as the ground the coaching relationship stands on.

It also matters to me that you know what you can expect from coaching, what is my responsibility and what remains yours.

I respect your decisions and your autonomy.

I do not take on the role of a therapist, an adviser, or someone who would know better than you.

For me, an important part of responsible coaching is continually reflecting on my own work, knowing the limits of my competence, and being willing to seek professional support or point you elsewhere when coaching is not the right kind of help.

The beginning can be just a conversation.

You do not need to know how many sessions you need, or to name precisely what you want to change.

To begin, it is enough to know that you want to look at something differently.
